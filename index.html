Your numbers
Why 1,500 calories
and how it works.
Your estimated TDEE
~2,100 kcal/day
78kg × 1.55 activity multiplier (strength training 4–5 days + moderate daily steps). This is roughly what you burn on a typical day.
Your deficit
~600 kcal/day
This puts you on track to lose approximately 0.5–0.6 kg per week — a sustainable rate that protects muscle mass. Going faster risks muscle loss and hormonal stress.
Why protein is so high
140g protein (1.8g/kg)
On a calorie deficit, your body looks for energy from anywhere — including muscle. High protein prevents this. It also keeps you fuller for longer, which makes the deficit easier to maintain.
Carb strategy
Cycling by day type
Training days get more carbs (~130g) to fuel performance. Rest days get fewer carbs (~90g) since you don't need the glycogen. Total calories remain 1,500 either way.

Nutrition
Your daily
meal plan.
Training day total: 1,500 kcal · P 140g · C 130g · F 47g. Slightly higher carbs to fuel your session and help with recovery.
Meal 1 — Breakfast
~370 kcal · P 35g · C 30g · F 12g · Time: 7:30–8:30am
· 4 whole eggs scrambled in 1 tsp ghee (minimum oil)
· 2 medium whole wheat chapatis OR 2 slices whole wheat bread
· 1 cup homemade sabzi — minimal oil (bhindi, carrot, beans, cauliflower or spinach)
· Black coffee or green tea (no sugar, no milk) — optional
Eat within 30–45 mins of waking. Skipping breakfast on a deficit leads to worse food choices later in the day.
Meal 2 — Mid-morning snack
~150 kcal · P 18g · C 8g · F 5g · Time: 11:00–11:30am
· 1 cup curd (150g) — plain, no sugar
· OR 2 boiled eggs with black salt and pepper
· 1 small apple or pear (optional — only if hungry)
This keeps hunger in check before lunch. Do not skip this if you train in the evening — it stops you from overeating at dinner.
Meal 3 — Lunch
~420 kcal · P 42g · C 45g · F 12g · Time: 1:30–2:00pm
Choose one protein:
· 150g grilled or tandoori chicken (no gravy — dry preparations are best for fat loss) best choice
· 180g fish (rohu/katla — boiled or minimal oil curry)
· 4 whole eggs (bhurji or boiled)

+ 1 cup white rice OR 2 chapatis (not both at lunch)
+ 1 cup moong or masoor dal
+ 1 cup sabzi (same chopped batch — no extra oil)
+ Skip curd at lunch — save it for snack
Gravy curries at restaurants use 3–4 tbsp oil per serving — this alone can add 300+ kcal invisibly. Stick to dry, grilled, or minimal-oil preparations when outside.
Meal 4 — Pre-workout 30–45 min before gym
~200 kcal · P 25g · C 20g · F 4g · Time: 5:00–5:30pm
· 1 scoop whey protein in 200ml water
· 1 banana OR 1 small bowl poha (light carbs for energy)
· Skip peanut butter here — save fats for other meals on a deficit
Do not train fasted. You need some carbs 30–45 min before lifting to maintain strength and intensity on a deficit.
Meal 5 — Post-workout Dinner Biggest protein meal
~360 kcal · P 40g · C 27g · F 14g · Time: 8:30–9:30pm
Choose one protein (avoid repeating lunch protein):
· 200g chicken breast — grilled, baked, or air fried recommended
· 4–5 eggs (bhurji or curry with minimal oil)
· 180g fish (steamed or grilled — not fried)

+ 1 chapati only (no rice at dinner on a deficit)
+ 1 cup dal OR 1 cup sabzi
+ 1 small bowl curd (100g)

Dinner is your post-workout recovery window. Protein here is the most important — do not reduce it even if not very hungry.
No rice at dinner. The one chapati is enough carbs for overnight recovery. Removing dinner rice alone saves ~130 kcal and helps fat loss without affecting performance.
Rest day total: 1,500 kcal · P 140g · C 90g · F 55g. Carbs are lower since you don't need to fuel a training session. Fats go up slightly to compensate and keep you satiated.
Meal 1 — Breakfast
~350 kcal · P 33g · C 22g · F 15g · Time: 8:00–9:00am
· 4 whole eggs scrambled in 1 tsp ghee
· 1–2 whole wheat chapatis only (reduce from training day)
· 1 cup sabzi — minimal oil
· Black coffee or green tea — no sugar
On rest days, no urgency to eat early. Eating slightly later naturally reduces the hunger window and helps maintain the deficit more easily.
Meal 2 — Mid-morning
~160 kcal · P 12g · C 8g · F 9g · Time: 11:30am–12:00pm
· 1 small bowl curd (150g) — plain
· Small handful of mixed nuts — 10–12 almonds or walnuts
· Skip fruit on rest days — save carb budget for meals
Meal 3 — Lunch
~400 kcal · P 42g · C 35g · F 14g · Time: 1:30–2:30pm
Choose one protein:
· 150g grilled chicken best choice
· 4 whole eggs
· 180g fish (minimal oil)

+ 1 cup white rice (smaller portion than training day)
+ 1 cup dal
+ 1 cup sabzi
Rest day lunch is where most of your carbs sit. Keep rice to 1 cup only — not heaped, just level.
Meal 4 — Evening snack
~130 kcal · P 13g · C 5g · F 7g · Time: 4:30–5:30pm
· 2 boiled eggs with black salt and pepper
· OR 50g paneer cubes with chaat masala
· Black coffee or green tea (no sugar)
On rest days this replaces the pre-workout snack. Keep it light and protein-focused — no banana or starchy carbs needed.
Meal 5 — Dinner High protein, low carb
~460 kcal · P 40g · C 20g · F 20g · Time: 8:00–9:00pm
Choose one protein:
· 200g chicken breast — grilled or baked recommended
· 5 whole eggs (bhurji with veggies, minimal oil)
· 200g fish (grilled or steamed)

+ 1 chapati only (no rice on rest day dinner)
+ Large bowl of sabzi (bhindi, broccoli, spinach, beans — cook with less oil)
+ 1 small bowl curd (100g)
Rest day dinner is higher fat than training day — that's intentional. Healthy fats from eggs, ghee, and curd keep you satisfied through the night without spiking blood sugar.
Food notes
Eat freely (low cal, high volume)
All vegetables (raw or cooked with minimal oil) · Cucumber · Tomato · Lettuce · Mushrooms · Black coffee · Green tea · Lemon water · Buttermilk (chaas, no salt added)
Good protein sources
Chicken breast (grilled/baked) · Eggs · Fish (rohu/katla/pomfret) · Curd · Dal · Paneer (limited — high fat) · Whey protein
Avoid completely
Fruit juices and soft drinks · Biscuits and namkeen · Restaurant gravy curries · Biryani and fried rice · Bread (white/maida) · Sweets and mithai · Alcohol · Packaged "diet" snacks (usually high sugar)
Cooking oil rule: Maximum 2–3 tsp total oil per day across all meals. Use a teaspoon to measure — do not eyeball it. Most fat loss diets fail because of invisible cooking oil calories.
If hunger hits between meals
5 safe options
under 150 kcal.
1. Boiled eggs (2)
With black salt and chaat masala. Quickest option. Prep in advance and keep in fridge.
~140 kcal · P 12g · Zero carbs · Very filling
2. Plain curd (200g)
No sugar, no fruit. Add a pinch of roasted jeera and black salt. Cool and filling.
~120 kcal · P 8g · Probiotic · Low GI
3. Roasted makhana (1 bowl)
Dry roast with salt and pepper. Crunchy and satisfying. Better than any packaged snack.
~100 kcal · Low fat · Low GI
4. Cucumber + tomato salad
Sliced cucumber, tomato, onion with lemon juice, black salt, and chaat masala. Unlimited quantity — almost zero calories.
~30 kcal · High volume · Kills hunger fast
5. Black coffee or green tea
No sugar, no milk. Suppresses appetite and boosts fat burning slightly. Limit to 2–3 cups per day total.
~5 kcal · Zero fat · Natural appetite suppressant
Hunger management rule: On a 600 kcal deficit, some hunger is normal and expected — especially in the first 2 weeks. Drink water first (hunger is often thirst). If still hungry after 15 mins, eat from the snack list above.
